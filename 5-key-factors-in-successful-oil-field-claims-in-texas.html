Act Fast After the Accident

Get same day medical care, report the injury in writing and photograph the scene. Rigs move, equipment gets repaired and crews rotate, so evidence fades within weeks.

More Than One Company Can Be Liable

Operators, drilling contractors, service crews and equipment makers often share fault. Each one carries its own insurance, which can raise the total recovery available.

Know What Your Claim Covers

Medical treatment, lost wages, lost earning capacity, pain and suffering, and in cases of gross negligence, punitive damages. Most Texas claims must be filed within two years.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What should I do right after an oil field accident in Texas?

Get medical care the same day, report the injury to your supervisor in writing, photograph the scene and your injuries, and collect witness names and numbers. Avoid recorded statements and do not sign anything before a lawyer reviews it.

 

2. Can I sue my employer after an oil rig injury in Texas?

Often, yes. Texas does not require most employers to carry workers’ compensation. If your employer is a non-subscriber, you may be able to bring a negligence claim directly against them.

 

3. Who can be held responsible for an oil field explosion?

Responsibility can fall on the well operator, the drilling contractor, service or wireline companies, site safety supervisors, and the manufacturer of defective equipment such as a valve or blowout preventer.

 

4. What compensation is available after an oil rig injury?

Claims can include past and future medical costs, lost wages, lost earning capacity, pain and suffering, mental anguish, disfigurement and physical impairment. Punitive damages may apply when a company acted with gross negligence.

 

5. How long do I have to file an oil field injury claim in Texas?

Generally two years from the accident date for injury claims, and two years from the date of death for wrongful death claims. Illness from chemical exposure can follow different timing.
